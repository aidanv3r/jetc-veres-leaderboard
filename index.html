How the New Score Is Calculated

The number shown next to each player (or pairing) is a weighted average of their most recent 10 results. All 10 results are shown in the pop-up window. Results older than the 10 newest do not count.

1. Base points for a result

Set categories:

  • Decisive set: loser held to 0, 1, or 2 games (6-0, 6-1, 6-2)
  • Competitive set: 6-3, 6-4, or 7-5
  • Contested set: 7-6

Single set or partial set — total 20 points, with a smaller spread than a full match (10 / 6 / 2):

  • Decisive win → 15  |  Decisive loss → 5
  • Competitive win → 13  |  Competitive loss → 7
  • Contested win → 11  |  Contested loss → 9

Full match (best of 3 sets) — total 20 points, spread 12 / 8 / 4:

  • Decisive match — won in straight sets with at least one decisive set → win 16 / loss 4
  • Competitive match — won in straight sets with no decisive set but at least one set that was not a tiebreak set (for example 6-3, 6-4 or 7-6, 6-4), or decided in a tiebreak/third set in a match that included a decisive set → win 14 / loss 6
  • Contested match — no decisive set in the match, and either the match was decided in a tiebreak/third set or every set went to a tiebreak (for example 7-6, 7-6) → win 12 / loss 8

Partial sets are classified by the games reached: 5-2 is decisive; 5-3 or 4-3 is competitive. If a practice session ends with the sets split, each set is scored as its own single-set result.

Forfeits: a voluntary forfeit (quitting) is a decisive win/loss for the format in progress — 15/5 for a set, 16/4 for a match — no matter what the score was when play stopped. An injury forfeit keeps the score at stoppage, and that score is classified by the normal rules.

League multiplier: every league result is multiplied by 1.5 after base points are assigned. Practice results use the base value with no league multiplier.

Line multiplier (league only): a league result is also multiplied by the line actually played — #1 line ×1.10, #2 line ×1.00, #3 doubles ×0.90 — for both sides, win or lose, so playing a higher line earns more for a win and keeps more points in a loss. "Actually played" means the line of the opponents faced, which is the player's own line except when courts are switched. Practice results have no lines and no line multiplier.

Drills are not results and are never counted.

2. Recency weighting (most recent 10 only)

Only the 10 newest results for that player or pairing are used.

Position weights (newest → oldest): 1.0, 0.95, 0.90, 0.85, 0.80, 0.75, 0.70, 0.65, 0.60, 0.55.

Time decay: if a result is more than 8 weeks (56 days) old at the update date, its position weight is multiplied by an additional 0.85.

Final weight for any result = position weight × time decay.

3. Opponent strength (practice only)

For practice results, points are adjusted by opponent strength using a 0.15 coefficient:

multiplier = 1 + 0.15 × (opponent score − player score) / 10

The scores used are calculated from earlier results only (a rolling reference): a result can never change its own adjustment, and no future result is used. League results do not receive an opponent multiplier. On the Doubles boards, opponent strength uses the opposing pairing’s score; if that pairing has no earlier score, the average of the two opponents’ individual-doubles scores is used, and if that is also unavailable the multiplier is 1.0.

4. Final score

Score = Σ (points × final weight) ÷ Σ (final weight)

Players and pairings are ranked by this score, highest first. If a player or pairing has fewer than 10 results, the average is taken over the results that exist. Frequency of play does not add points.

5. Movement arrows and earlier results

In the New boards view, green ↑ or red ↓ compares the new rank to the rank on the current published board.

Spring 2026 results may be shown in a player’s pop-up window under “Earlier results — not counted in this rating.” They are history only: they have no points and never change a score, a rank, or an adjustment.

Individual Doubles ranking

The Players sub-tab under Doubles ranks individuals by their results across pairings.

For each doubles result, start with the pairing’s points (after the league multiplier or practice opponent adjustment). Each partner’s credit is then adjusted by partner strength using a 0.15 coefficient:

credit = pairing points × (1 + 0.15 × (player score − partner score) / 10)

The two partners’ credits average to the pairing’s points — they are not split so that they add up to the pairing total. This keeps the individual board on the same scale as the pairing board. The player and partner scores used are calculated from earlier results only.

Each player’s individual score is the weighted average of their credits across their most recent 10 doubles results, using the same position weights and 0.85 time decay as the other boards.
