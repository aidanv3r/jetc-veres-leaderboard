How the Score Is Calculated

The number shown next to each player (or pairing) is a weighted average of their most recent 6 results.

1. Base points for a result

Set categories (used for single sets and to classify matches):

  • Decisive set: 6-0, 6-1, 6-2
  • Competitive set: 6-3, 6-4, 7-5
  • Contested set: 7-6

Single set (practice or league):

  • Decisive win → 14  |  Decisive loss → 4
  • Competitive win → 12  |  Competitive loss → 6
  • Contested win → 10  |  Contested loss → 8

Full match (best of 3 sets):

  • Decisive match — won in straight sets with at least one decisive set → win 16 / loss 4
  • Competitive match — won in straight sets with no decisive sets, or won in 3 sets with at least one decisive set → win 14 / loss 6
  • Contested match — won in 3 sets with no decisive sets → win 12 / loss 8

Partial sets (practice only): scored by the same set cutoffs using the final game score that was reached. A 5-2 is decisive; a 5-3 or 4-3 is competitive; a 7-6 partial is contested.

League multiplier: every league result is multiplied by 1.5 after base points are assigned. Practice results use the base value with no multiplier.

Forfeit: treated as a decisive win (14 for a set, 16 for a match) for the winner and a decisive loss (4) for the loser.

2. Recency weighting (most recent 10 only)

Only the 10 newest results for that player or pairing are used. Older results drop out of the calculation entirely.

Position weights (newest → oldest):

  • Most recent result: 1.0
  • 2nd: 0.95
  • 3rd: 0.90
  • 4th: 0.85
  • 5th: 0.80
  • 6th: 0.75
  • 7th: 0.70
  • 8th: 0.65
  • 9th: 0.60
  • 10th (oldest in the window): 0.55

Time decay: if a result is more than 8 weeks old, its position weight is multiplied by an additional 0.85. Results 8 weeks old or newer receive no extra decay.

Final weight for any result = position weight × time decay.

3. Opponent strength (practice only)

For practice results, points are adjusted by opponent strength using a 0.15 coefficient:

multiplier = 1 + 0.15 × (opponent score − player score) / 10

The multiplier applies to both wins and losses. League results do not receive an opponent multiplier — they use base points × 1.5 only. On the Doubles boards, opponent strength uses the opposing pairing’s score.

3. Final score

For each of the (up to) 6 results:

  1. Assign base points from the outcome definitions above.
  2. Apply the league multiplier (×1.5) if it is a league match.
  3. Multiply by the final weight (position weight × time decay).

Score = Σ (points × final weight) ÷ Σ (final weight)

Players and pairings are ranked by this score, highest first. If a player or pairing has fewer than 6 results, the average is taken over the results that exist (using the same position weights starting from the newest). Frequency of play does not add points — only the quality and recency of the results that do exist.

4. Movement arrows

Green ↑ or red ↓ compares the current rank to the rank stored at the last update.

Individual Doubles ranking

The Players sub-tab under Doubles ranks individuals by their contribution across pairings.

For each doubles result, the pairing’s total points (after league multiplier) are split between the two partners and adjusted by partner strength using a 0.15 coefficient:

adjusted = base × (1 + 0.15 × (player score − partner score) / 10)

The two adjusted values are then normalized so they sum to the pairing’s total. Each player’s individual score is the weighted average of their adjusted shares across their most recent 6 doubles results, using the same position weights and 0.85 time decay as the main boards.
